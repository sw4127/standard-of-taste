Standard of Taste · an instrument, not a quiz

What your ear lets through

A web instrument that measures which categories of audio flaw you catch and which get past you — in cents of pitch drift, milliseconds of timing error, kilobits per second. A number about your own taste that you can be wrong about.

Why it exists

Two findings the incumbents cannot serve

The project began with user interviews conducted for a Columbia Business School product engagement with Tidal. Two of them did not fit the product being built, and became this one instead.

  • Past listening predicts less than present and forming taste. Recommendation systems are built almost entirely on play history — a record of who you were.
  • Almost nobody can describe their own taste in words. Nearest-neighbour and embedding models encode taste as coordinates. Those coordinates are machine-readable and not human-readable, so the system can act on your taste while you still cannot articulate it.

Both point at the same gap: measure what a listener can currently discriminate, and return it in language.

Method

Hume, operationalized

David Hume's Of the Standard of Taste (1757) argues, against the relativist position, that aesthetic judgment admits of a standard — one located in the qualified observer, whom Hume characterises by five discriminable qualifications: delicacy, practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice, and good sense.

The engineering move is to treat those five as operational constructs and build a performance task for each — tasks with objectively verifiable answers, where the respondent can be demonstrably wrong. That replaces self-report, which measures self-image, with measurement against ground truth. Three of the five have working instruments.

Freedom from prejudice · built

The Prestige Test

Rate clips blind, then rate the same clips with artist names and acclaim attached. Movement between passes is prestige bias, corrected by each respondent's own drift on unlabelled control items — a within-subject design requiring no external ground truth. Some labels are deliberately false; every deception is disclosed on a mandatory debrief screen.

Delicacy of taste · built

The Delicacy Trials

Two recordings of one passage; one carries a defect applied at a controlled magnitude. Forced choice, objectively scoreable. Confidence is captured per trial, which yields a calibration curve — Hume's "good sense" as arithmetic rather than assertion.

Delicacy, measured · built

The Threshold Test

An adaptive staircase converging on the smallest defect you can reliably detect, reported in physical units. Not a rank — you caught a 12-cent detune and missed a 40-millisecond smear.

Depending on why you are here

Four ways in

If you have thirty seconds

Open the Prestige Test and rate sixteen short music clips. The debrief at the end tells you which of the names you were shown were invented, and by how many points your own rating moved toward them. That moment is the product.

If you assess products

The interesting decisions are the deletions. A six-tier ranking was removed after it classified respondents correctly only 30.5% of the time, replaced by an interval wide enough to be true. A priced tier was removed because charging for the training loop would have put the honest deliverable behind a wall. A predecessor product was discontinued on measured demand of 29 visitors.

Every open decision was classified by severity and reversibility: reversible choices advanced on a stated default, while one-way doors — pricing, data schema, deletions — halted for explicit sign-off.

If you assess analysis

The Lab is the honest part. It carries a metric dictionary that prints "no defensible target yet" where a target cannot be justified, an instrument-health panel, a parameter-recovery page validating the psychometric pipeline against simulated responses with known parameters, a page of the measured limits the instrument could not fix, and a data model stating everything the product stores and every event it sends.

It also carries a registry of falsified hypotheses — every belief this project tested and had to abandon, with the measurement that killed it. The list is not curated: an automated check reads the engineering record and fails the build if an entry is missing, because a self-reported list of failures you were free to edit is worth nothing.

There are no respondents yet, so every figure derived from simulation is badged SIMULATED in the product itself. The pipeline — item response theory, signal detection, calibration and reliability — is real and tested; the normative data is not there, and the site says so rather than implying otherwise.

If you assess engineering

No language model touches a verdict. A fixed-weight scoring engine and an authored, version-controlled knowledge base compute every result in code; where a model is used at all it is confined to prose, enum-locked, at low temperature on a pinned snapshot, cached by input hash. Roughly 250 cached generations cover 3,456 result permutations, bounding worst-case lifetime inference cost near one dollar, with transient failures excluded from the cache so a fallback can never be pinned.

Share links carry the respondent's raw answers and every surface recomputes from them, so an edited URL can only display what the engine would genuinely conclude. All audio is public-domain or Creative Commons, each item carrying a captured licence proof and a SHA-256 of its source file; continuous integration fails if either is missing.

Where it went, and where it is going

The blueprint

The instruments measure well and report in units. Units are not a description of anybody — so the measurement was turned into language, by comparing a respondent to themselves across fault families rather than to a population that does not exist yet. Since each defect was applied at a known magnitude, that contrast is ground truth and requires no cohort. That layer has shipped, and with the comparison reading every one of Hume's five criteria now has an instrument behind it.

Shipped output — a sentence the product renders, verbatim

Stated plainly

Nobody has used this yet, and distribution was deliberately out of scope. The project was built to test whether a rule set could hold an AI engineering agent to production quality across three months, more than 340 commits and more than 2,000 automated tests — not to find an audience. All five of Hume's criteria now have an instrument, and each page states what its instrument cannot tell you rather than implying it settles the question.

Constraints

What it refuses to do

  • No personality inference. It measures a performance and makes no claim about the person.
  • No percentiles. There is no cohort, so there is no honest rank to report.
  • No language model in the scoring path. Two respondents with the same answers get the same result, always.
  • No points, streaks or leaderboards. Refused by standing rule, not by oversight.
  • No number the data cannot support. This rule has deleted shipped work, which is the only evidence that a rule of this kind is real.
