## E6 — the commissioned measurement, and what it changed

### Prestige Test: the POOL is the constraint, not the session length
`SD = 11.0/sqrt(n)` percentage points, fitted, residuals inside 0.4. **Still falling when the
pool runs out** — the 8th clip is one of the most valuable in the set. Verdict agreement 89.3%
overall, **73.3% for people within 5 points of a ±15% line**, and no reachable length fixes the
second number. PM ruled RT-103a (a): grow the scored pool toward ~14 (5.1 → 8.1 min).

### Delicacy: the verdict was wrong nearly half the time
Six tiers placed a person correctly **30.5%** of the time at 15 trials. No granularity rescues
it — two bands reach 70.2% against Prestige's 89.3%. Cause is resolution: SD 0.106 against a
narrowest tier of 0.125, a band **1.18 SD wide**. Both criteria (cohort alpha 0.70, tier width
2 SD) land on **~42–45 trials = 21 min**, which is the session 15 was chosen to avoid.

**Resolution: RT-90a's rule applied — report the band, never the point.** The tiers are gone;
`detectionBand` reports a guessing-corrected Wilson interval. **It takes 12 of 15 to beat the
coin at 95%.** Copy went through Cowork (PM rates my prose the weaker) and is wired verbatim
but for one factual fix at the 12/15 boundary.

Numbers regenerate into `docs/analytics/e6-*.txt` on every test run.

## FALSIFIED — do not re-derive

1. **"The 8th prestige clip makes the test worse."** Artifact of redrawing item parameters per
   length. Nested design: 89.1% → 89.2%, flat.
2. **"Per-person drift sets the prestige precision floor."** Drift ON and OFF are identical to
   two decimals — `pct` measures shift toward the label and the pool's up/down balance cancels
   it. Kept as a regression test.
3. **"Information per minute is the right criterion for a fixed-form test."** It is constant by
   construction; every length scores identically. Derive from the CLAIM instead (R3 discipline).
4. **"The /vs question renders at 1.11:1."** My probe read `background-color` only; FluidField
   paints its light field as a **gradient**. The page is fine.
5. **"The bias card overflows."** Measured: it does not. Nor does delicacy. Only the threshold
   card did, because its hero is 150px against their 76px.
