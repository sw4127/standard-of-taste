## THE RULE TRACK P RUNS ON, AND IT IS NARROWER THAN IT LOOKS

**A citation may describe the MEASURING APPARATUS. It may never describe how well people score.**

Pitchfork's mean of 7.0 is a fact about an institution's use of its own ruler — it ranks nobody.
"Trained listeners detect about five cents" is a population statistic about human performance, and
printing it beside a reader's threshold manufactures the norm this product refuses to have at n = 0.
Guarded in `standards.test.ts` and `apparatus.test.ts`, not merely stated.

## FALSIFIED — do not re-derive

1. **"A published critics' ranking can set the spread over the clips the Prestige Test already plays."**
   Checked before building, against the sources rather than from memory. The pool is CC0/public-domain
   repertoire chosen for licence clarity, and **no enumerated critic ranking covers it**: Michael
   Tanner's ranked twenty-one Beethoven works (BBC Music Magazine) contains none of our works, and
   **four of the fourteen scored clips are Creative Commons artists no critic has ever ranked**. The
   consequence is RT-I3(a): critics supply the reference SCALE, never the spread over our items.
2. **"The BBC Music Magazine list of fifteen Chopin works is a ranking we can use for spread."**
   It would have been ideal — short piano works where a twenty-second excerpt is a fair sample, all
   available from a CC0 complete-works set we already licence, one performer throughout. Opening the
   article's own framing killed it: **it is numbered but not ordered by merit**, with no countdown
   and no statement of greatness. Using its numbers as a spread would have been inventing a ranking.
3. **"Eleven of eleven degrees is a meaningful ceiling for the degrees-used count."**
   Rating sixteen clips at random lands on **8.606 distinct values of eleven** — the top of the scale
   is reachable by indifference, and two simulated listeners with entirely different stability both
   scored 11 of 11. The engine now carries the null model and no surface may print the count without
   it.

## FINDINGS THAT MUST NOT BE LOST

1. **A GUARD PINNED TO A VALUE EXPIRES SILENTLY WHEN THE VALUE CHANGES.** The pool-size guard
   matched a digit or the single number word the pool happened to be when it was written. RT-103a
   grew the pool, the copy began spelling the new count, and **the guard passed while eight surfaces
   stated a size it could not see.** Widened to enumerate number words to twenty. Assume other
   value-pinned guards in this repo have expired the same way.
2. **A FIX THAT LIVES IN A FUNCTION IS ONLY A FIX FOR THE CALLERS THAT USE IT.** S4 removed a
   duplication from the critic line; S6 reproduced it exactly by composing the line on the page
   instead of calling the deck. Two commits apart.
3. **THE LOWERCASE-SENTENCE DEFECT HAPPENED FOR THE THIRD TIME.** A spelled count opened a sentence.
   The remedy now lives beside the hazard — `numberWordLeading` in `numbers.ts` — because the fourth
   caller will not remember. A guard for it exists in two decks.
4. **A DECORATIVE FLOURISH THAT ASSERTS A FACT IS STILL AN ASSERTION.** Two false claims reached the
   flagship sentence of Track P: that MUSHRA and Pitchfork are "a century apart" (they are
   contemporaries) and that both cluster near the top of their scales (measured for one, unsourced
   for the other). **Neither carried a digit, so no guard would have caught either.** Found by
   reading the rendered line.
5. **I CLAIMED A CITATION WAS VERIFIED WHEN IT WAS NOT.** The Track P analysis said the ITU
   recommendation had been "verified by opening" it; a web search had returned a summary and a URL.
   Opening it properly corrected a figure — MUSHRA's scale is **0–100 continuous**, not 1–100. The
   correction is recorded in the document rather than swapped.
6. **A MEASUREMENT FROM A DEGENERATE VIEWPORT IS NOT EVIDENCE.** Twice the browser pane returned a
   plausible number from a viewport I did not have: `clientWidth: 0` produced a false overflow
   report, and `resize_window` with `preset: "mobile"` reported success while the page measured
   785px. **Always print the viewport beside the measurement.**
7. **THE STALE CLAIM WAS WIDER THAN THE BRIEF SAID, AGAIN.** The brief named one page promising the
   streaming-import comparison; it was in **six** places including FAQPage structured data, both
