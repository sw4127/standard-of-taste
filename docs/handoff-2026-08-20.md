## THE HEADLINE FINDING: a lossy level could not be a dB figure

This is the thing that must not be re-derived.

`lossyLadderForSource` labelled each level with the damage measured at **one** window (@75 s). Across
all nine windows pb1 would serve, a fixed **128 kbps** does:

```
  @1s   @27s  @53s  @79s  @105s @131s @157s @183s @209s
  1.94  1.85  1.75  1.71  1.46  1.94  1.41  1.81  1.70   dB   -> 1.376x
```

against a `MAX_CROSS_WINDOW_RATIO` of 1.15. `assignInstances` cycles those files **inside one
session**, so "level 1.796 dB" would have meant anything from 1.41 to 1.94 — the defect `driftMs`
mode was introduced to fix at the seed level, reappearing one layer up. It is **worse at the top of
the ladder**, which is the wrong end: 32k agrees to 1.04x, 64k 1.17x, 128k 1.38x, the 320k anchors
1.68x — while a staircase spends its trials near the threshold.

**PM ruling RT-85a (a):** label in **kbps** (exact across windows by construction, the same
resolution RT-74a took for timing), select by dB (so steps stay distinguishable), and **state** the
damage variation rather than hide it. D4's own wording names kbps for this family.

**What is NOT claimed (N3):** a kbps threshold is a property of listener **and** material. It is
survivable only because a lossy session stays on one source (RT-65), and the source is named when
the threshold is reported.

**THE AXIS IS INVERTED.** Damage rises as the label falls. `FAMILY_AXIS` in `staircaserender.mjs`
gives `ladderMonotone` its direction. Any new consumer reading `measured.value` must take direction
from the family rather than assuming "up".

### Two floors, both measured from the render, both fitted to it
- **`MEASURED_LOSSY_FLOOR_KBPS`** = `{pb1:160, pb4:192, pb6:112}`. Five ladders came back
  non-monotone, every inversion at the gentlest end. `rungs.mjs` already knew the shape ("above
  ~128 kbps the curve is NOT MONOTONE… two of five sources reverse below 1.7 dB") and set its
  *nominal* range to start at 2.0 dB — but `lossyLadderForSource` never applied that and built down
  to 0.42 dB. Computed from 450 real measurements instead of that blunt constant: a flat 2.0 dB
  floor would have cost pb1 12→7, pb4 10→6, pb6 7→5.
- **`LOSSY_EXCLUDED_WINDOWS`** = `{pb4:[1,53,106]}` (RT-86a a). The pre-registered anchor gate fired
  on those windows — pb4's gentlest levels measured **less damage than their own window's
  transparency anchor**. They are the quiet opening of an Adagio, where there is little
  high-frequency content to discard so anchor and damage converge. pb4 keeps its full 192k→32k
  ladder on six windows rather than a truncated 80k→32k ladder on nine; both cost 54 clips.

**BOTH ARE CIRCULAR and this is the top open risk.** They were derived from the very render they now
constrain. Nothing re-derives them if the pool is re-rendered with a different seed or a codec
update, and nothing detects that they have gone stale. They are pinned constants with `MEASURED_` in
the name.

## FALSIFIED — do not re-derive
1. **"The 3-cent pitch floor applies to the measured p95."** It is a **parameter-domain** floor
   (`rungs.mjs`: `param 3 -> measured 2.7`). A ramp peaks at 0.95 of its parameter, so level 3.1
   *predicts* 2.94 cents — under a 3-cent measurement floor by construction. The first Layer A run
   flagged pitch 3.1 on 8 of 9 windows; that was a unit mismatch, not a finding.
2. **"`lossyStepCollapses` is superseded by the monotonicity floor."** Monotone means *increasing*;
   `MIN_LOSSY_LEVEL_RATIO` means increasing *enough to be two levels*. 4 of 174 adjacent steps rise
   by less than 1.15x (worst pb6@0s, 112k→80k, **1.053x**). Now wired into `computeKnownLimits`,
   **reported not gated**, on the RT-85a precedent.
3. **"Lossy gets a third of the variety the other families do."** True when lossy locked to one
   source's three windows. RT-84a gave every lossy source nine of its own; pb6 now **matches** the
   pooled families and only pb4 (six windows) is worse. Measured: worst repeat pooled 2, pb6 2, pb4 3.
4. **"pb4 is the best-behaved lossy source."** True of its @75 s curve; it is the **worst** at the
   gentle end across nine windows. One window's curve does not describe a source.

## Architecture the next session must not break

- **`eligibleWindows(family)` lives in `src/engine/staircase-pool.ts` and is the ONLY sanctioned way
  to ask which instances a family may use.** It intersects the renderer's `instanceWindows` (RT-75a
  trajectory gate) with `layerA.excludedWindows` (fitness + floors). Reading `instanceWindows`
  directly is a bug. A Layer A exclusion against family `"*"` blocks the window for **every** family
  — the reference failed, and the reference is the A side of every trial there.
- **`sessionInstances(family, lockedSourceId?)`** narrows to one source; lossy **must** pass one.
- **The pipeline does not own this.** `staircasevalidate.mjs` no longer exports `eligibleWindows`;
  nothing in `src/` may import a build script.
- **`staircase-validate` reads magnitude from the manifest, measures fitness fresh, and hashes every
  file against the manifest first.** That hash is what makes reading safe.
- **Clipping is READ, never re-measured** (RT-17a) — invisible after loudnorm. References get theirs
  from the source window (RT-81a).
