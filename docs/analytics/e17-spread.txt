--- 5. WHY EVERY HISTORICAL TRANSFER WAS REFUSED (derivation, not a measurement) ---
US sound recordings published from 1926 onward are protected for 100 years from publication
(Cornell's public-domain chart, "Sound Recordings Published in the United States", checked
2026-09-04). As of 2026 only recordings published BEFORE 1926 are public domain there.

Candidates found for works on the ranking, with a public-domain tag on archive.org or Commons,
and the year each was published:

  Symphony No. 7      Stokowski / Philadelphia          1927   PD in 2028
  Missa solemnis      Toscanini                         1934   PD in 2035
  Hammerklavier       Schnabel                       c. 1935   PD in 2036
  An die ferne Gel.   Raucheisen edition                1937   PD in 2038
  Emperor Concerto    Serkin / Walter / NY Phil         1941   PD in 2042

Every one is still in copyright; the earliest misses by a single year. One archive.org item
states its reasoning outright -- that works published before 1978 got 75 years -- which is a rule
about printed works and not about sound recordings.

Separately, IMSLP marks a large body of recordings "Public Domain - Non-PD US": free where the
site is hosted, restricted in the United States. A licence read as "Creative Commons Zero" was in
full "Creative Commons Zero 1.0 - Non-PD US", on a 1953 Deutsche Grammophon recording. That is why
the pool's licence guard matches exact allowed values rather than blocking known-bad substrings:
the failure mode was a good prefix with a bad suffix.
