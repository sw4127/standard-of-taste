## THE THREE QUESTIONS THE PM ASKED, ANSWERED

### 1. The negative bias — inherent, and therefore correctable

Decomposed EXACTLY, from the stationary distribution of the rule's Markov chain (no
sampling error), cross-checked against 100,000 brute-force reversals from the real
`recordResponse`:

```
  A  rule + reversal-averaging, endless ladder, infinite run    -0.25
  B  the ladder has ends                              -0.02 .. +0.50
  C  the run stops at 12 reversals from a fixed start -0.02 .. +0.17
  D  the N3 ladder-end guard's selection               0.000 .. +0.021
```

**Term A is a CONSTANT** — −0.25 steps across a fifty-fold range of step-size-over-slope,
varying by 0.006 across the six real conditions. The printed number is a flat ~8.5% too
sensitive, every family, every listener.

**FALSIFIED — do not re-derive it:** the hypothesis that the bias tracks step-size-over-slope.
It was pattern-matching on terms B and C moving while A held still. A fine ladder is still
biased.

**ALSO FALSIFIED:** that the N3 ladder-end guard's censoring caused `timing, sensitive`'s
+0.38. The guard is innocent (D ≤ 0.021). The cause is **term B = +0.499** — that listener
sits near the timing ladder's 12.5 ms floor and truncation shoves the reversal mean up.

### 2. The acceptance criterion — RMSE was the wrong gate for both claims

- **Cross-sectional claim.** The gate is CI coverage. **Ours was 49.3–71.8% against a
  nominal 95%** — a false statement beside every result (N3). `fitThreshold` fixes it.
- **Longitudinal claim.** A retest is a DIFFERENCE and a constant bias CANCELS in a
  difference, so the arc is limited by precision σ, not RMSE. `MDC = 2.772σ`,
  `MDC80 = 3.960σ`. Inverting for a one-ladder-step change gives **σ ≤ 0.252**.
  **Measured σ is 0.71–1.29 — 2.8× to 5.1× too noisy**, needing 328–1053 trials against
  the 38 it has. **The one-step-per-retest claim is dead at any viable session length.**

Trade curve (pitch, average): 12 reversals/38 trials/16 min detects 3.60× · 20/62/26 min
detects 2.55× · 50/152/63 min detects 1.93×.

### 3. The lapse rate — not the cause, but it found a bigger error

Procedure bias barely moves (+0.02..+0.15, real in only 3/6 by paired test). But the
**CLAIM bias** — against the ear rather than the ear-plus-mis-clicks — moves **+0.25 to
+0.52 steps at a 6% lapse, t = 7.9 to 11.3, six of six.** ~20% worse than the person's
actual ear.

**The trap:** at λ≈6% `pitch, average` reads +0.02 and looks unbiased. It is −0.35 and
+0.38 cancelling at one lapse rate in one condition. Same λ gives +0.76 elsewhere. **Any
correction tuned at one lapse rate makes another worse.**

## R4 — what `fitThreshold` did and did not buy

**The pre-registered criterion was NOT met.** It required beating the reversal average on
bias, RMSE *and* coverage in all 18 cells; it wins 37 of 54 cell-metrics. Every miss is on
bias or RMSE.

**Why, so nobody re-runs this experiment:** the staircase spends ~38 trials in a two-to-three
level band, so the SLOPE is close to unidentified, and integrating over a nuisance parameter
you cannot pin costs about as much variance as curve-fitting saves. The reversal average
never estimates the slope at all — it is robust by declining to model, and it is a hard
baseline. **σ is 0.93 vs 0.94: a wash.**

**What it did buy, and why it ships anyway:**
- **Coverage 49–72% → 94–100%**, and **94–98% against a MISSPECIFIED listener** (Weibull
  generator, logistic fit). The interval is wide enough to contain the truth *despite* the
  residual bias, which resolves the bias honestly.
- **The ladder-end blow-up is gone.** `timing, sensitive` went from +0.35/+0.46/+0.78 across
  lapse rates to −0.10/−0.00/+0.21.

`estimateThreshold` is marked **SUPERSEDED for product surfaces** in its own header.
**E5 must call `fitThreshold`.**
