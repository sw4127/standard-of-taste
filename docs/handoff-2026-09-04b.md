## FALSIFIED — do not re-derive

1. **"Public-domain tags on historical recordings can supply a pool of critic-ranked works."**
   US recordings published from 1926 get 100 years, so as of 2026 only pre-1926 is public domain
   there. Every candidate found — Stokowski 1927, Toscanini 1934, Schnabel c.1935, Serkin 1941 —
   is still in copyright, the earliest by a single year. One archive.org item states a rationale
   that applies to printed works rather than to recordings.
2. **"The pool's bandwidth differences are explained by how many times its audio has been encoded."**
   Four of six sources were already lossy, so the clips were checked. **Three of six end in an
   encoder-shaped cliff — at 8624, 15967 and 17786 Hz — and they are not the lossy-sourced ones:**
   the widest came from an mp3 and one of the narrow ones from FLAC. Spread across the pool 10002 Hz.
3. **"The pool's brightness differences inflate the spread on the pairs the critic separated."**
   Expected, because the narrowest clip sits in three of the four far pairs. Measured the other way:
   **3758 Hz mean gap across far pairs against 6498 across close ones**, so the confound points
   toward the null — it makes a difference harder to find, not easier.
4. **"The top-scoring window of a recording is fit to put in front of a listener."**
   The scorer's first pick was **32% near-silent** for the Violin Concerto and sat across a **4.30s**
   gap between two Diabelli variations. The Diabelli failed all twelve candidates and passed at the
   twenty-third; the search was widened to sixty rather than the dead-air gate relaxed.

   question and without the listen gate arming**, then advanced. Keyboard focus and programmatic
   clicks go straight through. Use the `disabled` attribute. **BiasFlow looks like it has the same
   defect and does not** — it guards in the handler (`!played`), verified in a browser. I spawned a
   task against it on the strength of a class name and had to withdraw the premise.
